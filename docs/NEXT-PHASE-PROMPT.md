# Next phase: media hub, collections, AI annotation

This document has two parts. Part A is a feasibility assessment of the requested features against the current codebase. Part B is the implementation prompt to hand to a future session. Nothing here has been implemented.

---

## Part A. Feasibility assessment

### What exists today (baseline)

Family Album at `main`/`staging`: Next.js 16, Prisma 7 on Postgres 16, pg-boss background jobs, local photo storage, magic-link auth with ADMIN/MEMBER roles, trips with activities and GPS tracks, per-trip themes, per-trip visibility (PRIVATE, LINK, PUBLIC), photo linking, installable PWA. Every photo already records `uploaderId`, so "track which family member uploaded which photo" is already stored; it only needs to be surfaced in the interface.

### Feature-by-feature

| Requested feature | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Collections alongside trips, both shareable | **Straightforward** | New `Collection` and `CollectionItem` tables (many-to-many). Reuse the trip visibility model and share-cookie mechanism by generalising `canViewTrip` into a `canView(entity)` helper. A photo stays on at most one trip but can sit in any number of collections. |
| Short video clips uploaded to the album | **Moderate** | Store the original, transcode in a background job with ffmpeg to H.264 MP4 (1080p max) plus a poster frame and thumbnail. Adds ffmpeg (about 300 MB, unmeasured) to the Docker image and CPU time: a 90-second 1080p clip takes 2 to 5 minutes on a 2-vCPU VPS, a 4K HDR source 5 to 15 minutes, so clips show a "processing" poster until done. Phone HEVC/HDR video needs a tone-mapping step or it plays washed out. Limiting uploads to short clips keeps files small enough for the existing single-request upload, so no chunked-upload protocol is needed; a failed clip upload restarts from the beginning, and resumable uploads remain a non-goal. |
| Longer videos on YouTube (unlisted), embedded in albums | **Straightforward** | A media item that points at a YouTube video ID instead of a file. Title and thumbnail come from YouTube's oEmbed endpoint (no API key); the thumbnail is copied into our storage so grids never call YouTube. Playback uses the privacy-enhanced `youtube-nocookie.com` embed behind a click-to-play poster, so no Google cookies are set until someone presses play. Unlisted videos embed fine; private ones cannot be embedded at all. |
| "Flash videos" | **Defined** | Short clips (about a minute or less) that play inline and autoplay muted in grids, like stories or reels. These are the only videos uploaded to the album itself; longer videos go to YouTube. |
| Text context at upload, used for AI annotation | **Straightforward** | A review step after upload where the uploader adds free text (batch-apply to many files). A job sends the medium rendition plus the text plus known metadata to Claude with a structured-output schema and stores a description, tags, place, activity, objects, visible text, mood and a searchable summary. |
| Searchable pictures | **Straightforward** | Postgres full-text search over caption, annotation, tags, people and place names covers keyword search. Adding pgvector with text embeddings of the annotation gives "photos of us eating lobster on a boat" style semantic search. |
| Recognise pets | **Feasible for distinctive dogs and cats, not for look-alikes or chickens** | Face models ignore animals. Pet records with hand naming and naming from the upload text are cheap and cover most of the value. Automatic "Probably Biscuit?" matching needs a fourth model (an object detector plus whole-animal image similarity against confirmed photos, narrowed by species and lifespan, always with confirmation) and is treated as an optional extra. Chickens and other look-alike animals get a flock record rather than individual names. No consent issues. |
| Learn to identify specific family members, including today's adults as children | **Feasible, with one important constraint, one design requirement and a consent model** | Claude will not identify real people from their faces; that is prohibited by Anthropic's usage policy, and the model declines. Identification must be done locally with a self-hosted face-embedding model. The workable design: detect faces and compute embeddings on the server, cluster them, let the family name a cluster once, then auto-match new faces by similarity with a confirm/reject step. The uploader's text ("Grandma Jo at the lake") lets the app propose that an unnamed face is Grandma Jo. Names, once confirmed by the family, are passed to Claude as text so descriptions can mention them. Claude never does the recognising. Face embeddings do not bridge decades, so a person needs one face cluster per era, matched by age at capture; undated scans need an estimated era from the upload text and visible cues. |
| Suggest matching trips and collections on upload | **Straightforward** | A scoring function combining date-range fit, GPS proximity to a trip's photos and tracks, people overlap, tag overlap with a collection's title and description, and embedding similarity to the collection's centroid. Show the top three with one-click attach. |
| Track uploader | **Already stored** | Show "uploaded by" on cards and detail pages to signed-in members, add a filter, and a per-member count on the admin page. Uploader identities stay members-only, as today. |
| Network graph of similar images | **Moderate** | Needs image embeddings (a CLIP-style model). Precompute the nearest neighbours per photo into an edge table, then render with a WebGL graph library. Comfortable up to several thousand nodes; beyond that, show the graph for a trip, collection or person rather than the whole library. |
| Connect to Google Photos | **Two routes, both optional; no background sync is possible** | Since March 2025 the Google Photos Library API only exposes media an app itself uploaded, so library reads and sync are off the table. Day to day: the Google Photos Picker API (user picks items in Google's screen, app downloads them within about an hour; location metadata is stripped by Google). Backfill: Google Takeout archives, imported from a drop folder on the server, which keep originals, descriptions, album membership and GPS. OAuth consent screen required; "testing" mode with the family as test users avoids Google review at the cost of weekly re-consent. |
| Photos in several collections, attach after upload | **Straightforward** | Follows from the many-to-many model; bulk actions already exist in the gallery and extend naturally. |

### Architectural additions this implies

1. **An ML sidecar container.** Face embeddings and image embeddings are best run as a small Python service (FastAPI with InsightFace and OpenCLIP, CPU only) in a new `ml` compose service. This keeps the Node image lean and uses mature models. Cost: a 3 to 5 GB image and about 2 GB of resident memory with all models loaded; 2 to 4 seconds per image on a modest 2-vCPU VPS, which is fine in the background (roughly 10,000 photos overnight). **The sidecar needs its own 2 GB, so the full stack wants 4 GB of RAM with a swap file, or 8 GB without**; `docs/DEPLOY.md` currently promises 2 GB and must be updated. On a 2 GB server leave `ML_URL` unset: collections, video, annotation and keyword search still work, only faces, pets, semantic search, suggestions by similarity and the graph are hidden. A pure-Node alternative exists (ONNX models through `@huggingface/transformers` and `@vladmandic/face-api`) and avoids a second language at the price of weaker face models.
2. **pgvector.** Swap the database image to `pgvector/pgvector:pg16` (in compose and in the CI workflow) and add `vector` columns for face, image and text embeddings, each with its own fixed dimension. Prisma handles them as `Unsupported("vector(N)")` with raw SQL for nearest-neighbour queries.
3. **ffmpeg** in the app image and a `transcode-video` job for short clips only.
4. **Claude API** for annotation, with an `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` secret, off until an admin opts in, plus a per-upload opt-out.
5. **Optional reverse geocoding** (self-hostable Nominatim endpoint, off by default, left to the polish phase) if place names are wanted in annotations; Claude usually infers the place from the image and the trip title anyway, and this would be the only outbound flow that runs with annotation disabled, so it gets its own switch and its own question.

### Cost of AI annotation (order of magnitude)

Per image sent at the 1600-pixel medium rendition: roughly 2,500 input tokens for the image, about 1,000 tokens of cacheable instructions, and about 400 output tokens.

| Model | Approx. cost per image | 10,000 photos | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `claude-opus-5` | about $0.03 | about $300 | Best descriptions and judgement. Default per Anthropic guidance. |
| `claude-sonnet-5` | about $0.01 | about $100 | Very good for this task. |
| `claude-haiku-4-5` | about $0.005 | about $50 | Adequate for tags and short captions. |

The Message Batches API halves these prices for backlog annotation. Prompt caching makes the instruction block nearly free after the first call on Opus 5 (minimum cacheable prefix 512 tokens) and Sonnet 5 (1,024 tokens); on Haiku 4.5 the minimum is 4,096 tokens, so a 1,000-token block does not cache there. Videos send three or four frames, so cost three to four times a photo. Editing an item's context re-annotates it, and batch-applying context to 200 items is 200 calls, so the prompt annotates once after the review step rather than on upload. Faces, embeddings and search run locally and cost nothing per image. The model is a configuration setting so the choice stays with the owner; the prompt below defaults to Opus 5 and exposes the setting.

### Things to decide before starting (the prompt lists them as open questions)

- Whether sending photos to Anthropic's API for annotation is acceptable for this family; the prompt keeps it off until an admin opts in and lets individual uploads opt out.
- Consent for faces: detection itself off until an admin opts in on a disclosure screen; matching off for everyone until an admin turns it on per person, minors only on a parent's instruction; and how long unnamed faces (guests, bystanders) may be kept.
- Whether a Python sidecar is acceptable, or the ML must stay in Node.
- Server size: the full stack needs 4 GB of RAM; CPU-only inference is fine for a few thousand photos, and a 100,000-photo library wants a bigger box or a GPU.
- Whether automatic pet matching is wanted, or naming pets by hand and from the upload text is enough.
- Whether to set up a Google Cloud project for the Picker, and whether to backfill from Takeout.
- Whether place names looked up from an outside geocoder are wanted in annotations.

Overall: everything requested is buildable on the current codebase. The two pieces that change the shape of the system are short-clip transcoding (ffmpeg) and the ML sidecar with pgvector; long videos are delegated to YouTube, which removes the need for resumable uploads; the rest is schema and interface work. A realistic sequence is thirteen short phases, each a single session's work and shippable on its own, described in the prompt: the visibility model and collections first, then video, then the review screen and annotation, then the ML features, then the graph, with Google Photos last and optional.

### Goal

Turn the app from a trip album into the family's media hub. The centre of the admin side becomes **ingestion**: every family photo and video gets uploaded here, enriched with context, made searchable, and filed into trips and collections. The centre of the family side becomes **sharing**: trips and collections are both first-class, shareable things. Privacy rules that hold today keep holding on every new surface: anonymous visitors see only PUBLIC content; a share cookie unlocks `/share/<token>`, that trip's own `/trips/<slug>` pages and its media route but never the global front page, timeline, map or search; uploader identities are members-only.

### Product requirements

1. **Collections.** A collection is a named, described, themed set of media (photos and videos) that is independent of trips. A media item belongs to at most one trip but any number of collections. Collections have the same visibility levels as trips (PRIVATE, LINK, PUBLIC; the database default is PRIVATE) with share links that can be rotated, a cover, and the same theme picker. Collections follow the trip rules exactly: only PUBLIC collections appear on the front page, in anonymous search and as a filter on the global timeline and map; LINK collections are reachable only through their own `/share/...` pages, which set `robots: { index: false, follow: false }`; from Phase 1a send `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin` app-wide and `same-origin` on trip and collection share pages (today only the `robots` metadata exists; `same-origin` everywhere would hide the referrer from keyed tile and style providers configured through `NEXT_PUBLIC_TILE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_MAP_STYLE_URL`, some of which validate it; in `next.config.ts` list the share-page entry after the `/:path*` entry, since the last matching header wins). Collection share pages reuse the trip pattern of putting the share token in the Open Graph image URL (now with `&kind=collection`) so link previews render; note in the README that this hands the secret link to whichever chat service generates the preview, which is inherent to previews of secret URLs. Collection pages have their own overview, items, timeline, map, settings and read-only share pages; item cards show collection chips. Members can create collections, add and remove items singly or in bulk from any gallery, the review screen, search results and the Unreviewed queue, and reorder items by drag or by date. Because media visibility is the union of its containers (see Architecture), one shared helper compares each affected item's effective visibility before and after a change and drives an **exposure warning** on all four paths that can change it: adding items to a collection, moving items to a trip (requirement 2, single and bulk), raising a container's visibility, and lowering one. Widening shows the exposure before confirming ("3 of these 12 items are in the private trip Lake House 2019; adding them to a PUBLIC collection makes those 3 visible to anyone on the internet") for every selected item whose current visibility (members-only when it has no container) is narrower than the target, and requires an explicit confirm. Lowering shows the inverse warning, because a union does not shrink by itself ("This trip is now private, but 3 of its photos are also in the PUBLIC collection Summer Favourites and stay visible to anyone on the internet") with a one-click "make these private everywhere" action. The warning distinguishes PUBLIC ("anyone on the internet") from LINK ("anyone holding the share link") and names the containers. Trip and collection settings pages show how many of their items are also reachable through another container, so the state is visible without triggering an action.

2. **Attach to a trip after upload.** Every gallery, the review screen, search results and the Unreviewed queue expose "Add to trip" (a move, since an item has at most one trip) next to "Add to collection" for single and bulk selections, reusing `bulkMoveToTrip`, with the exposure warning of requirement 1. Video items are eligible too.

3. **Video, two ways.**
   - **Longer videos live on YouTube as unlisted videos and are embedded.** Provide an "Add a YouTube video" action on trip pages, collection pages, the review screen and the global upload page. The member pastes a YouTube URL in any common form (`youtube.com/watch?v=`, `youtu.be/`, `youtube.com/shorts/`, `youtube.com/live/`, `youtube.com/embed/`, `m.youtube.com`, with or without extra query parameters). The server extracts the 11-character video ID, calls YouTube's oEmbed endpoint (`https://www.youtube.com/oembed?url=...&format=json`, no API key) to get the title, author and thumbnail URL, downloads the thumbnail into our storage and makes the usual renditions from it, and creates a media item of kind `EXTERNAL_VIDEO` with `provider = YOUTUBE`, `externalId`, `externalUrl`, `title`, `uploaderId`, and a date the member enters (defaulting to the trip's first day when added from a trip). Duration is filled in when `YOUTUBE_API_KEY` is configured (Data API v3 `videos.list` with `contentDetails`), otherwise left empty. Context text, annotation, people naming from the poster, suggestions, collections and sharing all work exactly as for uploaded media, so the video is searchable. Rendering uses a click-to-play facade: cards and the lightbox show our locally stored poster with a play badge and the note "Playing sends your request to YouTube"; only after the viewer taps does the page load an `<iframe>` from `https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/<id>?rel=0&autoplay=1` with `referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin"`. A weekly job re-checks oEmbed for each external video server-side: 401, 403 and 404 mark the item "no longer available" (deleted or made private), 5xx and timeouts leave it unchanged; the admin page has a filter for unavailable items. Editing lets a member replace the URL, retitle, or change the item's date. Deleting the item never touches YouTube. Explain in the interface that unlisted means "anyone with the YouTube link can watch", so the album's visibility does not extend to the video itself.
   - **Short clips are uploaded to the album.** Accept `video/mp4`, `video/quicktime` and `video/webm` (HEVC inside MOV or MP4 from phones is covered by the first two) up to `MAX_CLIP_SECONDS` (default 90) and `MAX_VIDEO_UPLOAD_BYTES` (default 1 GB, separate from the 100 MB photo cap; note in `docs/DEPLOY.md` that the Caddy `max_size 2GB` already allows it). The uploader reads the duration in the browser with a `<video>` element's `loadedmetadata` event and refuses over-long files before sending, with a friendly message naming the YouTube route; when the browser cannot decode the file (HEVC on many desktops fires `error` and `duration` is `NaN`) the duration is treated as unknown and the server decides; `MAX_CLIP_SECONDS` reaches the client through a runtime setting passed from the server component, not a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` build argument. The server re-checks with ffprobe in `transcode-video` and marks the item `FAILED` with the same message if the client check was bypassed. Keep the original, transcode in a background job to a web-playable H.264 MP4 capped at 1080p with a poster frame and a thumbnail, record duration and dimensions, and tone-map HDR sources. Clips show a "processing" poster until the job finishes, autoplay muted in grids on hover or tap and loop, and play with controls in the lightbox.

4. **Ingestion with context.** After files are uploaded and processed, the uploader lands on a **review** screen for that batch. There they can type free-text context for one item or for a selection ("Grandma Jo's 80th at the lake house, everyone came"), attach items to a trip and to collections, and mark the batch reviewed. Later phases add panels to the same screen: the AI annotation draft and estimated-date confirmation (Phase 3b), suggested trips and collections (Phase 4a), face naming (Phase 4b) and pet naming (Phase 4d); until then those panels do not exist, do not stub them. Context can also be added or edited later from the item page. Items that have never been reviewed are listed in an "Unreviewed" queue for admins. Annotation (requirement 5) is enqueued when a batch is marked reviewed, or by a scheduled sweep every five minutes that enqueues items where `annotatedAt IS NULL OR annotatedAt < contextUpdatedAt` (add `contextUpdatedAt` to `Photo`, set whenever `context` changes, so a later edit re-annotates after the same quiet period), not opted out directly or by inheritance (its own `annotationOptOut`, its trip's, or any of its collections'; a unit test asserts the sweep skips an item whose only opt-out is on its trip), and `updatedAt` older than `ANNOTATION_QUIET_MINUTES` (default 30), never on upload, so batch-applied context costs one call per item rather than one per edit. The review screen shows the countdown and a "don't send these to the model" action for one item or a selection.

5. **AI annotation.** A background job sends each new item (the 1600-pixel medium rendition for photos; the poster frame plus two or three evenly spaced frames for videos) to Claude together with the uploader's text, EXIF facts (date, camera, and a place name only when the optional geocoder is configured), the titles of the trip or collections it is attached to, and the names of confirmed people in it that may be sent (see requirement 7). Claude returns a structured record: a one-line caption, a longer description, tags, place, activity, objects, visible text, season and mood, a search summary, and, when the item has no reliable date, an estimated year range with a confidence and the evidence used (stored as `estimatedDate`, `estimatedDateConfidence`, `estimatedDateSource = MODEL`). Store the record on the item and mark it machine-generated until a member edits it. Keep the raw response in a separate `MediaAnnotationRaw` row, purged after `ANNOTATION_RAW_RETENTION_DAYS` (default 30) by a scheduled job and deleted with the item; never write prompts, context text or model responses to application logs, only the item id, model, token counts and `stop_reason`; refusals record the fact and a reason code, not content. Provide a "re-annotate" action and a bulk backfill for the existing library that uses the Message Batches API. Annotation is **off by default** and two gates must both hold before any request is sent: the operator sets `ANNOTATION_ENABLED=true` and `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, and an admin clicks "Turn on annotation" on a disclosure screen that lists exactly what would be sent (the rendition, the context text, EXIF date and camera, place name if geocoding is on, container titles, permitted people's names); the click stores `annotationOptInAt` and `annotationOptInById` in a single-row `AppSetting` table, the Privacy page shows both gates, and the admin can turn the opt-in off again without a redeploy. **Opting out**: `annotationOptOut` is settable on the upload form, on the review screen per item or selection, and on the item page at any time before the request is sent (setting it cancels any queued job); it is also settable on a trip and on a collection and inherited by their items (an item is opted out if its own flag, its trip's, or any of its collections' is set), shown on the settings pages next to visibility, so a whole trip can be marked as never leaving the server. Opted-out items are marked "not sent" and the entire request is suppressed, image, text and metadata alike. The **backfill** requires an explicit scope (a trip, a collection, a date range, or everything not yet annotated), shows the item count, the estimated token count and cost (from the dated rate table in the Annotation job decision, labelled approximate) and what will be sent, requires a typed confirmation, always skips items opted out directly or by inheritance and items already annotated, reports what it skipped and why, and can be cancelled, which stops submitting new batches. Never send an item Claude has already annotated unless the context changed or a member explicitly re-annotates.

6. **Search.** A search box in the navigation that searches captions, descriptions, tags, people, places, trip and collection names, and (for members) uploader. Keyword search uses Postgres full-text search over two stored `tsvector` columns and ranking: `searchVector` (caption, description, tags, place, trip and collection titles) is the only one queried for anonymous visitors; `searchVectorMembers` additionally covers every confirmed person's and pet's name, including minors and people whose `faceIndexing` is off (a member-entered name is not biometric data and the column is queried only for members; only people who opted out are excluded), and the uploader's name, and is queried only when the viewer is a member, so a name never enters the anonymous ranking path; the person facet for members lists every confirmed person, indexed or not (a unit test asserts an anonymous search for a seeded person's name returns nothing while the same query as a member returns their photos; that fixture seeds the name only in the `Person` row, never in a caption, context or annotation, and says so; a member search for a child seeded by that unit-test fixture, not by `prisma/seed.ts`, returns their confirmed photos). Semantic search (from Phase 4a) uses a text embedding of the annotation summary in pgvector, blended with the keyword score. Results show media cards with the matching snippet, generated only from fields of rows the viewer may see, and can be filtered by trip, collection, person, uploader, year and media type. Search respects visibility through `visibleMediaWhere`: anonymous visitors see only PUBLIC content, holding a share cookie grants no search access at all (a share cookie unlocks only that container's own pages and media route, never the global surfaces, as today), and filter facets are built from the viewer's visible set, so anonymous visitors see only PUBLIC trips and collections and no person or uploader facets. Search queries are never logged with the viewer's identity.

7. **People.** Detect faces and compute face embeddings for photos and video poster frames on the server, locally, using the ML sidecar described below. Cluster embeddings into unnamed people. Members name a cluster once (creating a `Person` record with name, optional relationship and birthday), after which new faces are matched by cosine similarity above a threshold and shown as "probably Sam" until confirmed or rejected; rejected matches are remembered as negative examples. The uploader's text is used to propose names: when the text mentions one known person and the photo has one unmatched face, propose that pairing. Each person has a page with their media over time. Do not attempt to identify people with Claude; it is not permitted and the model will decline.
   - **Detection is off until an admin opts in.** `FACE_INDEXING_ENABLED` defaults to **false**, and setting `ML_URL` alone never starts face detection (semantic search, suggestions and the graph run without it). Turning it on requires the operator flag plus an admin click on a disclosure screen modelled on the annotation one, which states that detection computes and stores a biometric face template for every face in every photo, including children, guests and people who will never be named, that templates stay on this server and are never sent anywhere, that unnamed ones are deleted after `FACE_UNNAMED_RETENTION_DAYS`, and that naming and matching still need a separate per-person decision; the click is recorded in `AppSetting`. The Privacy page shows the switch, the number of stored templates and the number of unnamed ones with their purge dates.
   - **Consent is recorded when a cluster is named, without a delete-and-recompute loop.** `Person.faceIndexing` defaults to **false**. When an ADMIN names a cluster, the form includes the birthday and the indexing toggle so consent is recorded in the same step; an adult whose birthday nobody knows can be recorded with an adult attestation (`adultAttestedById`, `adultAttestedAt`; label "I confirm this person is an adult and has agreed to face recognition"), which satisfies the minors check; the check passes only when a birthday is present and the person is 18 or older, or the attestation is set; clearing the attestation re-applies the null-out rule, and the People page shows whether each person is consented by birthday or by attestation. When a MEMBER names a cluster, the Person is created with indexing off and the cluster stays in the unnamed pool (subject to `FACE_UNNAMED_RETENTION_DAYS`) with a pending decision in a "Needs a decision" list on the admin People page; the embeddings are nulled when the admin declines or the retention period lapses, and the member form offers "this person is a child", which nulls the cluster's embeddings immediately while still recording the name. When admin naming ends with `faceIndexing` off (the admin declined, the person is a minor with no parent instruction recorded in the form, or there is neither a birthday nor an attestation), the app immediately sets `Face.embedding` and `FaceCluster.centroid` to NULL for that person, keeping the rows, boxes and CONFIRMED status as the non-biometric record of which media they appear in, and does no matching for them. Member naming is the one bounded exception: the cluster waits in the pending pool for an admin decision, capped by `FACE_UNNAMED_RETENTION_DAYS`, unless the member marks it a child, which nulls it at once. Only an ADMIN can turn `faceIndexing` on, recording `faceIndexingSetById` and `faceIndexingSetAt`; for a minor this is done on a parent's instruction and the form says so; there is no self-service path for a minor's faces. Enabling indexing later re-enqueues embedding for that person's confirmed faces. A nightly job adds people who have turned 18 to the "Needs a decision" list and never auto-enables them. When an admin creates a Person whose birthday makes them a minor, the form shows the indexing toggle with this explanation, and the People page lists people with indexing off so the choice stays visible.
   - **Opting a person out deletes**: their `Face` embeddings, their `FaceCluster` rows and centroids, all `PROPOSED` and `REJECTED` rows referencing them (negative examples are embeddings too), their person page, their name from every `annotation` JSON and from both search columns of affected media, in practice `searchVectorMembers`, where names live (re-run both triggers and recompute `textEmbedding` for every item whose annotation text changed, so the semantic index carries no trace either), and their name from the set passed to future annotation jobs. Confirmed appearances stay as a plain caption only if the person chose "keep my name on photos, stop recognising me"; the default removes both. **Turning detection off again** (the flag or the admin opt-in) stops detection and, on confirmation from the admin page, deletes all stored face embeddings, clusters and proposals; turning it back on re-detects from scratch. **Unnamed faces** (guests, bystanders) are purged with their clusters after `FACE_UNNAMED_RETENTION_DAYS` (default 180) unless attached to a named person, and the admin page offers "delete all face data" at any time. Deleting a media item deletes its faces, animal detections, embeddings and raw annotation rows in the same transaction.
   - **Names sent to Claude.** A confirmed person's name is included in an annotation request only when their `faceIndexing` is on and they are not a minor; otherwise the name is replaced with a neutral role word ("a child", "a family member") or omitted. An opt-out removes the name from future requests immediately. Face embeddings are never sent anywhere.
   - **Audience.** Person and pet pages, the people and pets index, face and animal boxes in the lightbox, name chips on cards and any JSON behind them are members-only: they call `requireUser`, are added to the `src/proxy.ts` matcher (as are the review screen, the Unreviewed queue, the graph page, collection settings and the Privacy page), and are `noindex`. This rule covers the face-derived identity layer: face and animal boxes, name chips, links to person and pet pages, the people and pets index, person facets, the members-only search column and any JSON carrying a `personId`; none of it reaches anonymous or share-link viewers, on PUBLIC content or anywhere else. Free text written by a member (caption, context) or by the model (annotation caption and description) may contain names and is shown according to the item's own visibility, because whoever wrote it chose to. A person page lists only media the viewer may see through `visibleMediaWhere`, even for members.

8. **Suggestions on upload.** For each new item compute a ranked list of candidate trips and collections and show the top three on the review screen with one-click attach. Signals: the item's date inside a trip's date range; GPS within a small distance of the trip's tracks or photos; people overlap with a collection's existing items; tag and text overlap with a collection's title and description; image-embedding similarity to the collection's centroid. Show why each suggestion was made ("same week, 2 km from the Ocean Path hike").

9. **People across ages.** Many photos will be of today's adults as children, often scanned prints without reliable dates. Face embeddings do not bridge large age gaps, so a Person owns several face clusters, one per era; naming a cluster of childhood faces after an existing person merges it under that person rather than creating a duplicate. Use the person's birthday and the photo's date to compute age at capture, and match new faces only against clusters from a similar age band. For undated photos, the estimated date from requirement 5 is shown for confirmation on the review screen and used by the matcher until a real date is set. Childhood matches use a stricter similarity threshold and always require confirmation, because siblings as children look alike. The uploader's text is the strongest signal for these photos, so the text-to-name proposal ("Sam at age 4") applies here too. All consent rules of requirement 7 apply unchanged: a person's childhood faces are covered by that person's own `faceIndexing` setting.

10. **Pets.** A `Person` record can be a pet (`kind = PET`, `species` from DOG, CAT, CHICKEN, HORSE, OTHER, optional `livedFrom` and `livedTo`). Pets are named by hand from the lightbox and by the text-to-name proposal from the upload text, have pages and are searchable like people, and the annotation job may use confirmed pet names (identifying animals is not restricted). A species with no individual matching (chickens, fish) gets a flock record ("the chickens"). Pets have no consent settings. **Optional, only if the owner answers open question 7 with yes:** the ML sidecar runs an object detector restricted to animal classes on each photo and poster frame; for each animal box compute an image embedding of the crop and compare it with the crops of that species' confirmed pet photos, narrowed by the pet's lifespan and by colour and breed words in the annotation; show "Probably Biscuit?" with confirm and reject, never auto-name, remember rejections, and propose the flock record whenever its species is detected.

11. **Uploader attribution (members only).** Show "uploaded by" on media cards, detail pages and the review screen for signed-in members only; anonymous visitors, including share-link holders, never see an uploader on any surface (cards, lightbox, detail pages, Open Graph tags, JSON routes, search results, graph payloads). Display the member's `name` and fall back to "a family member" when it is null; never render a member's email outside the admin page. The uploader filter on galleries (and on search once it exists) and the per-member upload counts on the admin page are members-only, and the member list behind the filter is not served to anonymous requests. The value is already stored as `uploaderId`; surface it.

12. **Similarity graph.** Compute a CLIP-style image embedding per item, precompute the nearest neighbours into an edge table, and provide a members-only graph page (whole library for admins, plus scoped graphs for a trip, collection or person) that renders nodes as thumbnails and edges by similarity using a WebGL graph library. Colour nodes by trip, collection, person or uploader; clicking a node opens the lightbox; a slider sets the similarity threshold. Cap the whole-library graph at a few thousand nodes and offer the scoped views beyond that. The JSON route first resolves the viewable node set with `visibleMediaWhere`, returns only edges whose both endpoints are in that set (never a redacted endpoint or a count of omitted edges), and requires view access to the scoping container. The same rule applies to any "similar photos" strip on an item page.

13. **Google Photos (optional, last).** Two import routes, no sync, each on its own answer to open question 8. Build the Takeout importer first because it needs no Google Cloud project and keeps locations; build the Picker only if the owner wants ongoing selective imports.
   - **Takeout import.** An admin-only importer for Google Takeout photo exports. Because archives are many gigabytes, read them from a server-side inbox directory (`IMPORT_INBOX_DIR`, a compose volume) rather than a browser upload; the admin page lists archives found there and starts an import job. The job streams each zip and pairs every photo or video with its JSON sidecar. Current exports name sidecars `<file>.supplemental-metadata.json` (older ones `<file>.json`), truncate the whole name to about 51 characters (`.supplemental-metadat.json`, `.supplemental-me.json`, `.supple.json`, `.s.json` and so on), and put a counter suffix after the extension (`IMG(1).jpg` pairs with `IMG.jpg(1).json`); match on the `<file>.` prefix plus any truncation of `supplemental-metadata` plus `.json`, and unit-test every form. Read `photoTakenTime`, `geoData` or `geoDataExif`, `description` and the containing album folder; dedupe against existing media by content hash and by Google id when present; set `takenAtSource = SIDECAR` and `gpsSource = SIDECAR` (new enum values, also added to the trusted sources in `geotag-photos`); turn each Google album folder into a collection, always PRIVATE with no share token regardless of the Google album's sharing state, and never read Google's sharing settings, face groups or shared-album participants; file everything through the normal processing pipeline. Report counts of imported, skipped and failed items and of collections created (noting they are private), and leave the archive in place until the admin deletes it; the admin page shows each archive's age and size and prompts to delete it once the import has been reviewed, since it is an unencrypted copy of the family's Google Photos history.
   - **Picker import.** An "Import from Google Photos" action on the upload page and the review screen. Use the Google Photos Picker API: create a picker session, open its `pickerUri` for the member (new tab on desktop, same tab on phones), poll the session at the returned `pollingConfig.pollInterval` (a duration string such as `"5s"`, parsed, bounded by `timeoutIn`) until `mediaItemsSet`, list the picked items, download each `baseUrl` with the `=d` suffix for photos and `=dv` for videos within the one-hour window, and feed the files into the existing upload pipeline (same review screen, notes, suggestions, faces). Store the Google media item id on the media row so re-picking the same photo is detected as a duplicate. Each member connects their own Google account with exactly the `photospicker.mediaitems.readonly` scope. Store refresh tokens in a `GoogleAccount` row keyed to the member, encrypted with AES-256-GCM under `TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY` (32 bytes base64, required when the feature is on, documented with rotation steps); a token is usable only by a server action running as the member who granted it, never by an admin or by a job acting for another member; provide "Disconnect Google", which revokes the token at `GOOGLE_OAUTH_BASE_URL` + `/revoke` and deletes the row, and delete it when the member is removed; tokens are never rendered or logged. Handle the 7-day expiry of testing-mode tokens by prompting to reconnect. Show a notice that Google removes location from API downloads and that the album will place the photo from a track if one covers it. Configuration: `GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_ID`, `GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_SECRET`; the feature is hidden when they are unset, and Takeout is hidden when `IMPORT_INBOX_DIR` is unset.
   - Explicitly out of scope: background sync with Google Photos, reading Google's face groups, and uploading from the album into Google Photos.

14. **Privacy disclosure.** From Phase 1b the README carries a "How the AI features work and what leaves the server" section and the app has a members-only Privacy page; every phase that adds an outbound flow (YouTube oEmbed and thumbnails, Anthropic, the geocoder, Google OAuth and Picker) extends both in the same commit, naming what is sent, which variable switches it off, and what is retained for how long, and the Privacy page shows each switch's current state.

### Assumptions (change these if wrong)

- "Flash videos" are short clips of roughly a minute or less; they are the only videos uploaded to the album. Longer videos are uploaded to YouTube by the member, kept unlisted, and embedded.
- Sending media to Anthropic's API is acceptable for this family once an admin opts in; the per-install switch and per-upload opt-out exist for those who disagree.
- A Python ML sidecar is acceptable in the compose stack, the server has at least 4 GB of RAM, and CPU-only inference is fine for the expected library size. On a smaller server the sidecar is simply not started.
- Face indexing is off for everyone until an admin turns it on per person. A person with no recorded birthday and no adult attestation is treated as not consented. Minors are indexed only when a parent asks an admin to enable it. Consent belongs to the person, not to the age they appear in a photo: an adult's setting covers their childhood photos too. Opting out deletes all biometric data for that person.
- Both Google routes are built only if the owner asks (question 8); when built, each is hidden until its configuration is set, so the decision needs no code change later.
- Face detection runs on all faces once an admin has opted in; unnamed faces are kept 180 days by default (question 4).
- Place names from an outside geocoder are not built unless asked (question 9).
- Automatic pet matching is built only if the owner asks (question 7); pet records and hand naming are always built.

### Architecture decisions

- **Schema (target state at the end of the last phase; add each column and table in the phase that first uses it).** Keep the `Photo` model and `/api/photos` route names to avoid a disruptive rename, add a `Media` type alias, and follow the existing naming (`photoId`, `photoAId`, `coverPhotoId`). Extend `Photo` with `kind` (PHOTO, VIDEO, EXTERNAL_VIDEO), `contentHash` (sha256 of the original, indexed), `sourceKind` (UPLOAD, GOOGLE_PICKER, TAKEOUT, YOUTUBE), `sourceId` (nullable), `provider` (YOUTUBE, nullable), `externalId`, `externalUrl`, `title`, `externalStatus` (AVAILABLE, UNAVAILABLE, nullable), `externalCheckedAt`, `durationS`, `videoRenditions` JSON, `context` text, `contextUpdatedAt`, `estimatedDate`, `estimatedDateConfidence`, `estimatedDateSource`, `annotation` JSON, `annotationModel`, `annotatedAt`, `annotationSource` (MACHINE, EDITED), `annotationOptOut` boolean, `reviewedAt`, `searchVector tsvector` and `searchVectorMembers tsvector` (both maintained by triggers; only the second carries people, pet and uploader names), `embedding vector(512)` (OpenCLIP ViT-B/32) and `textEmbedding vector(384)` (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), each with its own HNSW index. Add `SIDECAR` to `TakenAtSource` and `GpsSource`. Add `Collection` (slug, title, description, themeKey, visibility default PRIVATE, shareToken, coverPhotoId, createdById), `CollectionItem` (collectionId, photoId, position, addedById, unique pair), `MediaAnnotationRaw` (photoId, model, response JSON, createdAt), `Person` (kind HUMAN or PET, name, relationship, birthday, species nullable, livedFrom and livedTo nullable, faceIndexing boolean default false, faceIndexingSetById, faceIndexingSetAt, adultAttestedById and adultAttestedAt nullable, createdById; "is a minor" is derived from birthday and the attestation, not stored), `FaceCluster` (personId nullable, label, ageBandMin, ageBandMax, centroid vector(512)), `Face` (photoId, clusterId nullable, personId nullable, box, embedding vector(512), confidence, ageAtCaptureYears nullable, status DETECTED, PROPOSED, CONFIRMED or REJECTED, proposedPersonId), `AnimalDetection` (photoId, species, box, embedding vector(512), personId nullable, proposedPersonId, status as for Face; only with the optional pet matching), `MediaSimilarity` (photoAId, photoBId, score, unique pair with A < B), `GoogleAccount` (userId unique, encryptedRefreshToken, connectedAt), `AppSetting` (single row: annotationOptInAt and annotationOptInById, faceDetectionOptInAt and faceDetectionOptInById, faceDataDeletedAt). Add `annotationOptOut` to `Trip` and `Collection` as well. Keep `PhotoLink` for manual links.
- **Visibility.** Generalise `canViewTrip` into `canView(viewer, container)` for `{ kind: "trip" | "collection" }`, the share cookie into `share_<kind>_<id>` with `Viewer.shareTokens` keyed the same way, and `visibleTripsWhere` into `visibleContainersWhere`. **Media visibility is computed, not stored, and is the union of its containers**: `canViewMedia(viewer, media)` is true if the viewer is a member, or `canView` is true for the media's trip, or `canView` is true for any collection containing it; media in no trip and no collection is members-only. Add one `visibleMediaWhere(viewer)` Prisma helper and use it in every media query on every surface (galleries, timeline, map, search, graph, person and pet pages, JSON routes, Open Graph images); no surface builds its own media filter. `/api/photos/[id]/[size]` is the single enforcement point for all media bytes (photo renditions, video renditions and range requests, poster frames, YouTube posters): rewrite it to load the item's trip and collections and call `canViewMedia`; extend `?share=<token>` to `?share=<token>&kind=trip|collection`, accepted only when it matches the `shareToken` of a LINK container that actually contains the item; send `Cache-Control: public` only when the item is viewable by an anonymous visitor with no cookie and no share token (a PUBLIC trip or PUBLIC collection), never `immutable` for anything else, so rotating a token or lowering visibility leaves nothing in shared caches; range responses (206) carry the same headers and run the same check on every request. Rotating a share token immediately invalidates held cookies and `?share=` URLs.
- **Database image.** Switch compose and `ci.yml` to `pgvector/pgvector:pg16`; add the `vector` extension in a Phase 4a migration. Nearest-neighbour queries are raw SQL through Prisma with HNSW indexes.
- **ML sidecar.** New `ml/` directory with a FastAPI service (Python 3.12, CPU builds of onnxruntime, InsightFace `buffalo_l` for faces (research-licensed weights; say so in the README's AI section), OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 for 512-dimensional image embeddings, all-MiniLM-L6-v2 for 384-dimensional text embeddings, and, only with optional pet matching, an object detector restricted to animal classes) exposing `POST /faces`, `POST /embed/image`, `POST /embed/text`, `GET /health` and optionally `POST /animals`. Add it as the `ml` compose service: no host ports, attached only to an `internal: true` network that `app` and `worker` join in addition to the default network (they still need `db` and outbound hosts; only `ml` is internal-only); weights are fetched by a separate `ml-init` service (same image, `profiles: ["ml-init"]`, default network, entrypoint `python -m ml.download`) into the named `ml-models` volume, `docs/DEPLOY.md` documents `docker compose run --rm ml-init` as a one-time step before `docker compose up`, `/health` reports `models: missing` until it has run, and air-gapped installs copy the volume contents instead. Requests require a shared `ML_TOKEN` header, the sidecar rejects any request without it, and the app refuses to start with `ML_URL` set and `ML_TOKEN` empty. Nothing (image bytes, crops, embeddings) is written to disk or logs; `mem_limit: 2500m`; models loaded lazily and unloaded after `ML_IDLE_UNLOAD_SECONDS` (default 300); one request at a time. Each of `transcode-video`, `embed-photo` (image and text embeddings), `detect-faces` and, if built, `detect-animals` keeps `localConcurrency: 1`, and all four handlers also hold one in-process async mutex (`src/lib/jobs/heavy-lock.ts`) for the duration of their ffmpeg or sidecar call, so heavy work never overlaps inside the worker process; note in `docs/DEPLOY.md` that this holds only when a single process runs the worker, as the existing docs already require. The Node side talks to it with a typed client and treats it as optional: if `ML_URL` is unset, face, pet, semantic-search, similarity-suggestion and graph features are hidden and the rest of the app works. The sidecar's pytest suite runs in CI without weights by starting the app with `ML_STUB_MODELS=1` (deterministic fake embeddings and boxes) and checking every route's schema; a second test marked `@pytest.mark.models` uses real weights and runs only by hand.
- **Processing pipeline.** `processPhoto` today reads EXIF, resolves `takenAt`, reassigns the trip by date and enqueues `geotag-photos`; make it branch on `kind`: for `EXTERNAL_VIDEO` and video posters it only makes renditions and never touches dates, GPS or trip assignment. The upload route gains the video types in `ALLOWED`, in the `EXT_MIME` extension fallback (`mp4`, `mov`, `webm`, since browsers often send an empty or generic type for `.mov`) and in `EXT_BY_MIME` for the stored original's extension, and dispatches them to `transcode-video` instead of `process-photo`. `StorageProvider.getStream` gains an optional `{ start, end }` range and the media route gains video rendition keys.
- **Short clips.** Install ffmpeg (a build with libzimg, which the Debian package has) in the app image. New `transcode-video` job with `localConcurrency: 1`. Probe with ffprobe, transcode with libx264 `-preset veryfast -crf 22` and AAC audio, scale to fit 1920x1080, apply tone mapping for HDR (`zscale=t=linear:npl=100,format=gbrpf32le,zscale=p=bt709,tonemap=tonemap=hable:desat=0,zscale=t=bt709:m=bt709:r=tv,format=yuv420p`), extract a poster at 10 percent of duration and a thumbnail, and write `videoRenditions`.
- **YouTube embeds.** `src/lib/video/youtube.ts` with a pure `parseYouTubeUrl(url): string | null` (unit-tested against every URL form, including junk and non-YouTube hosts), an `oembed(id)` client with a short timeout and typed errors, and `fetchThumbnail(id)` that stores `maxresdefault.jpg` when it exists and falls back to `hqdefault.jpg`. A server action `addYouTubeVideo` validates with zod, requires a member, and enqueues `process-photo` on the stored poster in its renditions-only mode. A `YouTubeEmbed` client component renders the facade and swaps in the iframe on click. A `check-external-videos` job runs weekly via pg-boss `schedule`. Add a site-wide `Content-Security-Policy` (a `source: '/:path*'` entry in `next.config.ts` headers, since `frame-src` constrains the pages that render the facade and `frame-ancestors` is the app-wide clickjacking control) containing only frame-related directives: `frame-src https://www.youtube-nocookie.com; frame-ancestors 'none'; object-src 'none'; base-uri 'self'` (no `default-src`, so nothing else changes); the referrer policy is already set in Phase 1a. A full nonce-based policy is optional hardening in Phase 5, not part of the video work: a per-request nonce makes every page dynamic, and Next's own guide requires the nonce to be generated per request in `src/proxy.ts` (set `x-nonce` and the CSP header there, with the matcher widened to page routes while keeping the sign-in redirect only for today's protected paths, and static pages such as `/offline` made dynamic or served under a separate nonce-free policy through a path-scoped `headers()` entry and excluded from the proxy's CSP matcher, since browsers enforce the intersection of two CSP headers and `'unsafe-inline'` is ignored once `script-src` carries a nonce), `style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'` because `TripTheme` and other components render `style` attributes, `worker-src 'self' blob:` for MapLibre, `img-src`, `connect-src` and `font-src` including the hosts of `NEXT_PUBLIC_TILE_URL`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_MAP_STYLE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_MAP_GLYPHS_URL`, `form-action 'self'`, rolled out as `Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only` and enforced only once the e2e suite passes under it.
- **Annotation job.** Use the official `@anthropic-ai/sdk` with `client.messages.parse` and a zod schema through `output_config.format`. Default model `claude-opus-5`, configurable with `ANNOTATION_MODEL` (Opus 5, Sonnet 5 or Haiku 4.5). Put the fixed instructions in a cached system block (at least 1,024 tokens so it caches on Sonnet 5; note it will not cache on Haiku 4.5 below 4,096) and keep per-item content after it. For Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 send `thinking: { type: "adaptive" }` and `output_config: { effort: "low" }`; omit both for Haiku 4.5. Check `stop_reason` before trusting `parsed_output`: on `refusal` record the reason code and move on rather than letting the parse throw. The backfill estimate uses one table in `src/lib/annotation/pricing.ts` with a `pricesAsOf` date shown next to the figure: `claude-opus-5` $5 in and $25 out per million tokens, `claude-sonnet-5` $2 and $10, `claude-haiku-4-5` $1 and $5, batch submissions billed at half; assume roughly 2,500 input tokens per photo image (three to four times that for a video's frames), 1,000 instruction tokens (billed at the 0.1x cache-read rate on Opus 5 and Sonnet 5, at full input price on Haiku 4.5 where the block does not cache) and 400 output tokens per item, label the result approximate, and record actual `usage` per job so the admin page shows real spend next to the estimate. For the backfill, submit through the Message Batches API and poll for results; `messages.parse` does not apply to batch results, so validate each succeeded result against the same zod schema by hand and check its `stop_reason`, and do not count on cache hits inside a batch. Never include face embeddings or ask the model to identify anyone; pass permitted names as text only.
- **Reverse geocoding (optional, Phase 5, only if question 9 is yes).** `GEOCODER_URL` (a Nominatim-compatible endpoint such as a self-hosted one; the reverse call is `/reverse?lat=&lon=&format=jsonv2&zoom=`) and `GEOCODER_EMAIL`; when unset, annotation runs without a place name. Send a descriptive `User-Agent`, throttle to one request per second (the public instance's policy), round coordinates to 3 decimals before sending, cache results by rounded coordinate, and exclude a `GEOCODER_EXCLUDE` bounding box (the family's home area). Listed on the Privacy page and in the README section.
- **Search.** Two trigger-maintained columns as in requirement 6: `searchVector` over caption, description, tags, place, trip and collection titles; `searchVectorMembers` adding people's and pets' names and the uploader's name, queried only for members. "Permitted" here means members may see the name (every named person and pet, minus anyone who opted out), not the narrower rule in requirement 7 for what is sent to Claude; the two differ deliberately, since indexing keeps the name on this server. Blend keyword and semantic scores with one constant (`KEYWORD_WEIGHT = 0.7`); a unit test in `tests/unit/search/` runs 20 to 40 queries over seeded fixtures and asserts the expected item is in the top three; change the constant only when that test motivates it and say so in the commit.
- **Suggestions.** A pure function in `src/lib/suggest/` scoring candidates from the signals above, with unit tests, called after processing and again when context is edited; the embedding signal is skipped when `ML_URL` is unset.
- **Graph.** After embedding, upsert the eight nearest neighbours above 0.75 cosine into `MediaSimilarity`. Render with Sigma.js and Graphology, using `@sigma/node-image` for thumbnail nodes, on a client-only members-only page.

### Phases

Each phase is one session's work, is shippable, and ends with a commit, a push, green checks, and documentation updates including the README privacy section and the Privacy page. If a phase overruns, commit and push the shippable part with its tests and documentation and carry the remainder into a lettered follow-on phase (for example 3c) named in the commit message; never push a phase whose Playwright flow is red. Natural cut points: 1a front-page cards; 3b the scoped backfill; 4b the nightly minors job and the retention purge; 5 the nonce CSP and then the performance pass, with the graph shipping first.

**Phase 1a. Visibility model and collections.** `canView`, `canViewMedia` and `visibleMediaWhere`, the media-route rewrite, the collection schema, collection pages (overview, items, timeline, map, settings, share pages with noindex and referrer policy), create and edit forms, front-page cards. Playwright: create a collection, add photos from two trips, share it by link, view it anonymously.

**Phase 1b. Bulk actions, attribution, privacy page.** The exposure-warning helper on all four paths, add-to-collection and add-to-trip from trip galleries and the global timeline (search and the review screen gain the same actions when they exist), collection filter on the global timeline and map, uploader shown to members everywhere, uploader filter and admin counts, the README privacy section and the Privacy page. ### Definition of done
